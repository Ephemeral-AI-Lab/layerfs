## 1. T2 — grouping, re-measured on the current Store

### 1.1 What is grouped, and what it is worth — **measured**

Population: the **9,702 base-less whole-file records**, stored bodies **27,809,279 B**, canonical
**75,113,968 B** (2.701x), median record **4,778 B** decoded.

Grouped **within save boundaries** (packs are per save; every save cut lands on a pack boundary —
verified independently in B3 §1). Framing charged in full: 1 tag byte per record, the ordinary
lane's in-group grammar (4-byte count + one 4-byte end offset per record), and 4 B of whole-file
directory per group.

| target (sealing rule) | level | groups | frames | **total incl. framing** | **save** | median decoded group | single-record amplification |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 48 KiB stored (`GROUP_TARGET`) | 3 | 699 | 24,086,650 | 24,140,752 | **3,707,335** | 103,811 B | **21.7x** |
| 48 KiB stored | 19 | 699 | 20,694,705 | 20,748,807 | **7,099,280** | 103,811 B | 21.7x |
| 256 KiB stored (`PACK_LIMIT`) | 3 | 208 | 22,495,279 | 22,545,453 | **5,302,634** | 375,829 B | **78.7x** |
| 256 KiB stored | 19 | 208 | 18,870,481 | 18,920,655 | **8,927,432** | 375,829 B | 78.7x |
| 64 records | 3 | 254 | 22,658,997 | 22,709,539 | 5,138,548 | 288,145 B | 60.3x |
| 64 records | 19 | 254 | 19,074,403 | 19,124,945 | 8,723,142 | 288,145 B | 60.3x |

The sealing rule matters and it is the product's own: `GROUP_TARGET` is compared against the
**framed (stored) length the next record would produce** (`cas/selection.rs:56-70`), not against a
decoded size. The table above therefore seals on **stored** bytes. The B3 shape — cap the *decoded*
group at 256 KiB — is the more conservative variant and gives **4,631,493 B (L3)** /
**8,094,802 B (L19)** at a **48.5x** amplification; it is recorded for continuity, not used.

**Store after T2** (apparent, arithmetic on measured parts):

| config | apparent | vs v0.1.6 | gate |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| T2 @48 KiB, L3 (today's codec) | 54,042,169 | 1.0958x | FAIL |
| T2 @48 KiB, L19 | 50,650,224 | 1.0271x | FAIL |
| T2 @256 KiB, L3 | 52,446,870 | 1.0635x | FAIL |
| **T2 @256 KiB, L19** | **48,822,072** | **0.9900x** | **PASS (by 493,768 B)** |

**T2 closes the gate only in its most expensive form.** At today's codec level it recovers
3.7–5.3 MB of the 8,433,664 B gap and **still fails**.

### 1.2 What T2 costs

* **Access — measured.** One base-less record read goes from **4,778 B** decoded to a median
  **103,811 B** (48 KiB target, 21.7x) or **375,829 B** (256 KiB target, **78.7x**); the largest
  group decodes **744,029 B**. B3's 59.8x was the decoded-cap shape; the product's own stored-cap
  rule is worse, not better.
* **Attribution — holds today.** `space.whole_file_records` is fail-closed *because* the
  whole-file lane is the one lane with one record per group (`shared/space.py:268-277, 411-447`).
  Grouping makes per-object stored attribution impossible by construction; every per-object byte
  reading in every future receipt becomes an aggregate.
* **Memory — computed.** The group buffer rises from ≤ 262,144 B (one whole-file record,
  `pack_limit`) to ≤ **744,029 B** decoded (measured max) per in-flight group, plus the zstd
  window (pinned by `CodecProfile`, ≤ 256 KiB at this cutoff). **≈ +0.5 MiB per construction
  worker.** This is *not* the cost that matters.
* **Format — holds today.** The whole-file lane's directory is starts-only
  (`WHOLE_FILE_ENTRY_LEN = 4`) and the 8-byte `(raw_length, frame_length)` prefix is dropped at
  assembly (`WHOLE_FILE_COMPACT_DROP = 8`, `pack/assemble.rs:246-253`). Both assume one record per
  group. Grouping needs the seal arm changed (`cas/selection.rs:54-55`), the ordinary lane's
  in-group grammar reused, and `PACK_LIMIT`/`GROUP_TARGET` revisited → **a new physical format**.
* **LOC — est.** ~**150–350 production lines** across `cas/selection.rs` (108), `pack/assemble.rs`
  (261), `pack/layout.rs` (398) and `encoding/decode.rs` (207), plus tests. The machinery
  (`GroupCodec::Zstandard`, one frame per group) already exists for three lanes.

### 1.3 Verdict — T2

**Worth doing only as the last 5 MB, and only at level 19.** At today's codec it is 5.3 MB for a
new format, 78.7x read amplification and the loss of the only exact per-object byte instrument in
the harness. **§5.2 shows a cheaper way to get most of the same bytes.**

## 2. T3 — per-path version chains in large frames, re-costed

### 2.1 The number — **est**

B1 measured **27,184,431 B** of content at **13.682x** for a 371,937,306 B union, in **254 frames**,
mean **1,464,553 B**, max **11,263,931 B** decoded per read. The current Store's whole-file
canonical is **348,460,295 B**; the same ratio gives **348,460,295 / 13.682 = 25,468,000 B (est)**,
against today's **43,873,480 B** → **−18,405,000 B (est)**, Store ≈ **39.3 MB ≈ 0.797x (est)**.
This is the only tier that closes the gap with room to spare.

### 2.2 What it costs

* **A concept the Store denies — holds today.** `layerfs-storage/src/lib.rs:5-7` refuses a
  Workspace/history entity, and the schema has exactly four tables
  (`store_policy`, `object_packs`, `metadata_value_groups`, `objects`) — **no path anywhere**
  (`sql/schema.sql`, 83 lines). This is an **owner amendment** before design, not a refactor.
* **Access — measured (B1).** A single-version read inflates up to **11,263,931 B**; the mean frame
  is **1,464,553 B**. Today a base-less read decodes 4,778 B.
* **Memory — est.** The frames were produced with a 1 GiB window (`--long=30`); decoding is bounded
  by the frame, encoding is not. This is a memory decision, not a parameter.
* **Attribution — same loss as T2**, and worse: one group is one *path*, not one object.
* **LOC — est.** `layerfs-storage` is **6,408** production lines today (`encoding` 2,736,
  `cas` 1,707, `sqlite` 858, `pack` 798, `sql/schema.sql` 83). A path-keyed arrangement replaces
  the delta module's chain model *and* the whole-file lane's directory *and* the locator's meaning:
  **1,200–2,500 production lines (est)**, plus a C1-side correspondence API, plus a
  `SCHEMA_VERSION` bump. That is 20–40 % of the component.
* **It needs the same missing producer as the residual.** The path correspondence is Stage 7
  (#172), unbuilt — the identical blocker recorded in the T1 receipt.

### 2.3 Verdict — T3

**Not now.** It is the only lever with 18 MB in it, and it is 5–10x the cost of everything else on
this list combined. It is correctly deferred: the revisit condition ("T1's realised size lands
above the gate and the shortfall cannot be closed inside T1") is now *met*, so #189 should record
that **T3 is the named fallback if the cheap levers in §5 are ruled out** — not that it should start.

## 3. The ordinary lane — 344,456 B, and the tree-role bases that are not the reason

### 3.1 What is in the lane — **measured**, residual 0

```
72 groups · stored 1,022,795 B · decoded 3,027,326 B
  = 5,055 tree-role records (roles 3,5,7,8,9,10,13)   2,049,437 B
  + 1,738 inode-leaf records (role 6)                   950,429 B
  + group framing (4 + 4n per group)                     27,460 B
residual 0
```

**Negative (A2 reproduced, now with bytes):** **0 of 5,055** tree-role records carry a base, while
the producer attaches a predecessor to each changed page
(`layerfs-content/src/filesystem/sorted/page.rs:380-390`, `PredecessorProvenance::UnchangedPrefix`)
and the selector drops it **before** reading the advisory
(`encoding/delta/select.rs:204-222`). The finding is real.

### 3.2 What admitting them would buy — **measured models, negative result**

| model | bytes | ratio |
| --- | --: | --: |
| tree records in the **current** group partition, L3 (what the lane does now) | **619,520** | 3.31x |
| tree records, per record, L3 | 1,071,269 | 1.91x |
| tree records, per record + **previous same-role record** as a raw dictionary (proxy A) | 893,229 | 2.29x |
| tree records, per record + **best of the previous 20 same-role records** (proxy B, an oracle) | **783,045** | 2.62x |

**The negative result: grouping already beats every per-record dictionary these proxies can find.**
Proxy B is an *oracle* (it picks the best of 20 by trying them), and it still loses to the grouping
the lane already performs.

The structural reason is the record size: a tree-role record is **405 B** on average
(2,049,437 / 5,055), and a prefix record's fixed cost is **33 B** (tag + 32-byte base id,
`record_width`) — **8.1 % of the record**, against **0.4 %** for the whole-file lane's 7,937 B
records. The mechanism that pays on 8 KB objects does not pay on 405 B objects.

**Upper bound, and it is generous (est):** if every tree record hit the whole-file lane's based
ratio (**17.02x** measured), it would cost `2,049,437/17.02 + 33×5,055 = 120,414 + 166,815 =
287,229 B` — a saving of **≤ 332,291 B**, assuming a perfect predecessor for every record and
ignoring that the ordinary lane's grammar *cannot hold a prefix record at all*
(`encoding/full.rs:118-135` returns `Integrity("ordinary lane representation has no prefix
record")`). The realistic value is **≤ 0**.

**And the lane's gap is not base coverage.** Our lane decodes **2,999,866 B** against v0.1.6's
**2,013,482 B** (+986,384 B, +49 %): our tree pages alone (2,049,437 B) exceed v0.1.6's entire
ordinary lane (2,013,482 B), and our lane *additionally* carries the 1,738 inode-leaf records
(950,429 B) that v0.1.6 keeps in its pooled lane (1,636 records there).

### 3.3 Verdict — tree-role bases

**Not worth it, and it may be negative.** A2's observation is correct and its byte value is
≈ 0. The cheapest-looking item on the user's list is the one the measurements refuse. **Do not
open it.** If the ordinary lane is to shrink, the lever is *placement* (why 1,738 inode leaves are
in this lane, and why our pages are 49 % larger than v0.1.6's), not bases.

| no `object_role` | 3,629,056 | 886 | 56,844,288 |
| neither | 2,428,928 | 593 | 55,644,160 |
| `base_object_id` → **locator triple** (pack, group, record) | 2,748,416 | 671 | 55,963,648 |

**Marginal cost after repacking** (the honest axis — the repack alone is worth 434,176 B of the
table and is not attributable to a column):

```
base_object_id column   3,690,496 − 2,486,272 = 1,204,224 B   (294 pages)
object_role column      3,690,496 − 3,629,056 =    61,440 B   ( 15 pages)
both                    3,690,496 − 2,428,928 = 1,261,568 B   (308 pages)
locator triple instead  3,690,496 − 2,748,416 =   942,080 B   (230 pages)
```

**The brief's ~614,400 B for the column is low by half.** The measured figure is **1,204,224 B**.

### 4.2 The structural difference — **measured**, and it is not a mystery

v0.1.6's `objects` table is **2,650,112 B / 647 pages / 51,722 rows** and has **five** columns:
`object_id, canonical_length, pack_id, group_number, record_number`. It has **no `object_role`
and no `base_object_id`** — and it still stores **35,904 based whole-file records**. Our table
without the base column (**2,486,272 B**) is already **163,840 B smaller than v0.1.6's** with
**310 more rows**.

**holds today:** the base identity is *already in the packed record* for every lane that can carry
one — `record_width` is `1 + 8 + 32·has_base + frame` (`encoding/delta/record.rs:41-50`), the
whole-file lane's stored body is exactly `tag [+32 B base] + frame` (measured: 34,439 tag-1
bodies = 16,064,201 B, all 33 B + frame), and the pooled leaf carries its base too
(`POOLED_DELTA_TAG`, `encoding/pool/leaf.rs:1-40`). **The column is a duplicate.**

### 4.3 What moving it into the record would take — **holds today** + est

* the chain walk reads the base out of the locator: `encoding/delta/read.rs:164` and
  `encoding/pool/read.rs:233` both do `current.base_object_id` on an `ObjectLocation`;
* `base_object_id` is referenced in **9 files** (`cas/placement.rs`, `cas/selection.rs`,
  `encoding/delta/read.rs`, `encoding/delta/select.rs`, `encoding/pool/read.rs`,
  `sqlite/lookup.rs`, `sqlite/write.rs`, `sqlite/schema.rs`, `sql/schema.sql`);
* the change: parse the record's own tag+base before the next hop, keep the **same** PK lookup per
  hop (the base's role and location still come from `objects`), keep the same
  `DEPENDENCY_PACK_CACHE_BYTES = 4 MiB` bound;
* **bytes per read: unchanged.** The pack body that carries the base id is the body the reader
  already reads. No extra read, no extra byte.
* **LOC — est:** ~**150–300 production lines** in those 9 files, plus a `SCHEMA_VERSION` 4→5 bump.
  The current contract **rejects older Stores rather than migrating them**
  (`policy.rs:24-40`), so this invalidates every existing Store — the real cost of the item.
* The **locator triple** alternative keeps a table-side base pointer for **942,080 B** instead of
  1,204,224 B, and buys nothing the record-carried form does not: **prefer the record.**

### 4.4 Verdict — objects table

**The best bytes-per-pain item on the list after the codec level.** 1,204,224 B measured, no added
pack bytes, no access-cost change, contained blast radius, and the v0.1.6 lineage is the precedent.
It needs an owner ruling because it is a schema/format change.

## 5. What else is there — found, measured, ranked

### 5.1 The chunk lane is missing its bases — **1,737,621 B measured on identical content**

| | ours | v0.1.6 |
| --- | --: | --: |
| native-lane records | 1,098 | **1,098** |
| Σ raw_length | **22,032,441** | **22,032,441** |
| Σ canonical_length | **22,055,499** | **22,055,499** |
| records with a base | **0** | **650** |
| stored | **5,695,678** | **3,958,057** |
| gap | **+1,737,621** | |

**The two Stores hold the same 1,098 chunk records with the same payloads** (two sums matching
exactly over 1,098 records is not a coincidence), and v0.1.6 stores them in **69.5 %** of the bytes
because 650 of them carry a prefix base. This is the parent's *native lane +1,737,621 (20.6 %)*
row, and it is **entirely base coverage**.

**holds today:** the mechanism exists. The native lane's record grammar carries a base
(`PREFIX_TAG`, `record_width`), `compress_prefix` is the same code path the whole-file lane uses,
and the selector already has a chunk arm: *"A CHUNK object considers the first eligible listed
candidate and never the cache: its correspondence is already known by the caller"*
(`encoding/delta/select.rs:207-214`). **The caller declares no chunk candidate** — and the driver's own comment says why:
`construct_bytes` "builds `FinalizedObject::new(role, canonical)` with **no predecessors at all**",
and the driver can only attach one to the object it offers, the **content root**
(`ops/history.rs:1505-1520, 1705-1716`). This is exactly the shape of R0, one lane over. Whether the
correspondence can be produced harness-side (re-running CDC over the file's previous version) or
needs a C1 API change is **not settled here** and is the first thing to check.

**Verdict: the highest-value structural item after the codec level, and it needs no format
change.** 1,737,621 B measured, no read amplification (the frames stay per-record), no new entity.

### 5.2 The codec level — **~4,270,443 B est, no format change at all**

| population | level | stored | save | label |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --- |
| base-less, 9,702 records | 3 | 27,809,279 | — | measured (= the Store) |
| base-less | 9 | 26,486,347 | 1,322,932 | measured |
| base-less | **15** | 25,774,011 | **2,035,268** | measured |
| base-less | **19** | 25,692,037 | **2,117,242** | measured |
| based, 34,439 records | 3 | 16,064,201 | — | measured (= the Store) |
| based | 3 (model) | 15,724,431 | | model, 2.2 % low |
| based | 19 (model) | 13,615,712 | | model |
| based, L19 scaled to the Store's L3 | 19 | ≈13,911,000 | **≈2,153,201** | **est** |

**Total ≈ 4,270,443 B (est)** — half the residual — for a change to the compression level in
`CodecProfile`/`compress_prefix` (`encoding/codec.rs:260-300` pins `ZSTD_c_compressionLevel = 3`).

* **No format change.** zstd frames are level-independent; the decoder is unaffected; the window is
  pinned by the profile, not by the level; frames only get smaller, so no `frame_limit` is
  approached. Existing Stores stay readable and a Store written at 19 is read by today's code.
* **Bytes per read: unchanged.** Records stay per-record.
* **Memory: unchanged at read time** (the window is a profile parameter). Encoder working memory
  rises with level; **not measured here**.
* **CPU: not measured, and no timing number is offered** (shared machine; AGENTS.md §1). This is
  the item's whole risk: level 19 is a save-path cost over 348 MB of whole-file content, and the
  lane's complete command is already 41.0 s against a 15 s ceiling. Level **15** gives ≈4,170,000 B
  (est) of the 4,270,443 B for a materially cheaper codec.
* **Ruling:** a policy/profile bound, not a format — but it changes what every Store costs, so it
  should be ruled, not assumed.

### 5.3 VACUUM / repack — **843,776 B measured, 385,024 B net**

* our Store on a copy: 57,749,504 → **56,905,728** = **−843,776 B** (30 free pages = 122,880 B, plus
  106 pages of `objects` repacking);
* v0.1.6 on a copy: 49,315,840 → **48,857,088** = **−458,752 B**;
* **net against v0.1.6: 385,024 B.**

It is a close-time rewrite of a 57 MB file inside a phase that already exceeds its budget, it is a
product operation that needs a ruling, and it is not a tier. **Record it; do not lead with it.**

### 5.4 The group codec level — **261,819 B measured, one constant**

`GROUP_LEVEL = 1` (`encoding/codec.rs:57`). Recompressing the existing group payloads at the
existing group partition:

| lane | L1 (today) | L9 | L19 |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| ordinary | 1,022,795 | 966,287 | **875,547** (−147,248) |
| pooled-metadata | 2,019,419 | 1,974,694 | **1,904,848** (−114,571) |

**−261,819 B** at L19 (**−101,233 B** at L9), no format change, CPU only. L3 is *worse* than L1 on
the ordinary lane — the group window is capped at `GROUP_WINDOW_LOG_MAX = 16`, so the knob is not
monotone at small group sizes and must be measured, not assumed.

### 5.5 `object_role` — **61,440 B measured**, and not worth it

The role is needed **before** a record is read (it selects the lane), so it cannot move into the
record the way the base id can. 61 KB for a format change: **no.**

### 5.6 Pooled-lane grouping — **up to ~94,208 B**, marginal

`metadata_value_groups` + its autoindex cost **118,784 B** for **1,737 rows** against v0.1.6's
**24,576 B** for 468 rows, because the pooled lane seals on occupancy
(`cas/selection.rs:54-55`) — **1 record per group**. Grouping the pooled lane would recover most of
the 94,208 B difference and cost read amplification in the metadata lane. **Marginal; mention, do
not schedule.**

## 8. Negative results and residuals, in one place

1. **Tree-role bases are ≈ 0, and the oracle says so.** Best-of-20 same-role dictionaries (783,045 B)
   lose to the grouping the lane already performs (619,520 B). The 405 B record cannot carry a 33 B
   base id. **Retained.**
2. **T2 at the current codec level does not close the gate** (1.0635x at 256 KiB stored, 1.0958x at
   48 KiB). The tier only closes it when the level knob is *also* pulled.
3. **v0.1.6 also has 458,752 B of reclaimable free space**, so VACUUM's contribution to *the gap* is
   385,024 B, not 843,776 B.
4. **The brief's two borrowed figures are wrong on this artifact:** the column is 1,204,224 B, not
   ~614,400 B; the row count is 52,032, not 53,721.
5. **The PREFIX-frame model is not byte-exact** (113/400) and is 2.2 % optimistic; the level knob's
   based-half number is `est`, and the group-level numbers are measured on the *existing* partition
   only (a different partition would move them).
6. **Level 3 is worse than level 1 on the ordinary lane** — the group window cap makes the knob
   non-monotone; "higher level" is not a safe default.
7. **T3's 18.4 MB is an `est`** — it borrows B1's 13.682x measured on a 371,937,306 B union and
   applies it to a 348,460,295 B canonical set.
8. **No timing number appears in this note.** The CPU cost of every level change is **NOT
   MEASURED**, and it is the largest single unknown in §5.2.
9. **Residual 0** on: both Stores' dbstat-vs-apparent, both Stores' `bodies + framing = blobs`, the
   whole-file lane's tag split vs the lane total, the ordinary lane's `records + framing = decoded`,
   the codec model against the 27,809,279 B of base-less bodies, and the native lane's raw-length
   sums across the two Stores.
10. **Not run:** any stride3 confirmation (none exists), the lane itself, and the C1-side check of
    whether `construct_bytes` can return the chunk correspondence (§5.1).

## 9. One line per question

1. **T2** — re-measured on the current Store: **−5,302,634 B at the product's own 256 KiB pack size
   at today's level (1.0635x, still FAIL)**; **−8,927,432 B at level 19 (0.9900x, PASS)**. Cost:
   **78.7x** single-record read amplification, a new whole-file format, and the loss of the only
   exact per-object byte instrument. Worth it **only** as the final closer.
2. **T3** — re-costed: **≈ −18,405,000 B (est)** for **1,200–2,500 LOC (est)**, a new path entity the
   storage contract denies, and **up to 11,263,931 B decoded per read**. The only tier that closes
   the gap with room to spare; **not worth doing now**, and it should be *named* as the fallback in
   #189.
3. **The ordinary lane / tree roles** — **do not open it.** The producer exists; the prize is
   ≤ 332,291 B under a perfect oracle, and two measured proxy oracles are *worse than the grouping
   already in place*. The lane's 344,456 B gap is content volume, not base coverage.
4. **The objects table** — the split is exact: **1,204,224 B** is the `base_object_id` column,
   **434,176 B** is the un-vacuumed repack, **61,440 B** is `object_role`. v0.1.6 has **neither
   column** and still stores 35,904 based records, because **the base identity already lives in the
   packed record**. Moving it there costs ~150–300 lines in 9 files and a `SCHEMA_VERSION` bump, and
   **not one byte more per read**. **Worth it.**
5. **Found** — **the chunk lane's missing bases: 1,737,621 B measured on content both Stores hold
   identically** (v0.1.6: 650/1,098 records based; ours: 0/1,098), needing no format change; and the
   **codec level: ≈4,270,443 B (est) with no format change at all**, which is the cheapest half of
   the residual on this list.
