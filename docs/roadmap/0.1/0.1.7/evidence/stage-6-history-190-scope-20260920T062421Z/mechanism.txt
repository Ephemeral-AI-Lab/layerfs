## Reading

**Source fact.** The pooled reader is constructed once per resolved inode leaf
(`encoding/delta/read.rs`, `Resolver::resolve_charged`: `PoolReader::new()`), so
its pack cache - bounded at `DEPENDENCY_PACK_CACHE_BYTES` - never survives a leaf.
The decoded-group cache beside it is **operation**-scoped (`cas/read.rs`,
`ReadSession::groups`), and the two caches have the same job for the same access
stream: one holds decoded group bodies, the other the pack bytes those bodies
were cut from.

**Measured.** Two independent readings agree that the demands repeat:

1. The decoded-group cache is consulted *after* the pack body is acquired, so
   every hit is a demand the operation had already served for another record -
   at 0.951 (stride10) and 0.916 (stride3) of all record calls. Each hit still
   paid for the pack BLOB unless the same leaf had already fetched that pack.
2. The number of distinct packs a state can fetch is at most the number of packs
   its Store holds, so `fetches - packs` is a **floor** on fetches that re-read a
   pack already read inside the same state: 79,529 of 79,784 (99.7%) on stride10
   and 426,947 of 427,384 (99.9%) on stride3. The whole pack space is copied
   176x (stride10) and 444x (stride3) over within one run.

**Not a deeper-chain algorithm.** Chain edges per record call rise once, from 0
(the first states still write FULL records) to 0.28-0.39, and then stay there:
state 17 of stride10 reads 4.46 records per leaf against state 2's 2.00 - a 2.2x
structural rise - while bytes copied per leaf rise 54x (20 KiB to 1,085 KiB),
because fetches per leaf rise 3.8x and the bytes each fetch copies rise 14.2x
(10 KiB to 142 KiB) as packs mature toward `PACK_LIMIT`.

**What this does and does not establish.** It establishes that the term is
repeated acquisition of whole committed packs, discarded at the per-leaf
boundary, and not more algorithm per byte. It does **not** establish how much of
that repetition an operation-scoped cache can hold at the *existing*
`DEPENDENCY_PACK_CACHE_BYTES` bound: the repeats are many but their reuse
distance is not published by any counter. That is the treatment's question, and
the treatment is the experiment that separates the two readings: if the term is
scope, the same bound over a wider lifetime removes it; if the repetition is
wider than any bounded window, the counters move little and the reading is
refuted rather than confirmed.
